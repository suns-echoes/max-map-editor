Introduction

Welcome back M.A.X. Commanders!

This is the M.A.X. Map Editor - the open-source tool for crafting custom maps for the classic M.A.X.: Mechanized Assault & Exploration.

I started this project to fulfill our all-long dreams of assault and exploration of an unlimited number of new planets and regions where we could try new tactics and enjoy exploration like it was the first time. The original maps are great, but after almost thirty years we know them a little too well.

The main goal has not changed: an intuitive and enjoyable mapping experience for all M.A.X. enthusiasts - making a map should be a pleasure, not an exercise in hex editing.

The editor is a native Rust application (wgpu + winit) for Linux and Windows. It is portable - unzip it anywhere and run it; nothing is written to your system. Grab it from Releases, and see NEWS for what landed when.

What it does today

Maps in, maps out

  • - opens the original .WRL maps and exports game-ready ones - a round trip is byte-exact, proven against all 24 original maps;
  • - layered map projects with tile packs for all five terrains (green, desert, snow, crater, dark snow);
  • - the 24 originals ship as ready-to-edit starter templates;
  • - imports a foreign .WRL onto the tile packs you choose, so maps built elsewhere become editable projects;
  • - resize from any edge, and build a whole map out of any picture.

Drawing the ground

  • - tile painting with variant randomization, flood fill, and a sizable pencil and eraser (square or circle);
  • - the terrain brush - paint a land/water mask free-hand and let the editor build the terrain under it;
  • - auto-shore - water/land transitions draw themselves, beach and cliff ladder included;
  • - select, copy, cut, paste, and reusable templates of anything you built - rotated and flipped on the way down;
  • - passability editor with an overlay, and a validator that shows you the cells that would trouble the game;
  • - full undo/redo, all the way back.

Objects that stand on it

  • - the Scenery library - mountains, tree stands, cliffs, rocky outcrops and meadows as objects, cut out of the original art with the ground removed;
  • - placed by pixel, not by cell; nothing snaps to the grid, and neighbours share one shadow;
  • - four ways two objects can meet - normal, brighter, darker, and higher, which interlocks a hill with a mountain's flank by reading how tall each one stands;
  • - your own objects are welcome: draw on a transparent background and the editor snaps the colours to the map's palette;
  • - it all composites into the .WRL export - the game just sees ordinary tiles.

Whole worlds from a seed

  • - the random terrain generator: islands, continents, land masses or river-cut worlds, all from a seed you can share;
  • - adjustable water, obstruction and decoration balance, with optional symmetry for a fair start;
  • - obstructions arrive as whole formations - mountain ranges and forests, not single tiles;
  • - reroll until it looks right, and abort a run mid-way.

Colour, and how it will really look

  • - palette editor with live colour cycling, range retints, palette save/load and hot swap;
  • - in-game preview: cycling plus 6-bit colour, with an optional CRT effect for the full 1996 feeling;
  • - unit previews - stamp real units and buildings from your game data onto the map (team colours, turrets, shadows) to judge a palette against the art that will stand on it.

/!\ Saved games (experimental)

  • - opens a .DTA saved game as an ordinary editable map, with every unit, building, slab and piece of rubble standing where you left it, in its own team's colours;
  • - the Unit Properties inspector opens a picked object up: team, name, facing, orders, hits, ammo, cargo, and the unit's max values;
  • - paint the resource distribution - raw materials, fuel and gold - drawn with the game's own survey markers;
  • - writes them back: an untouched save comes out byte-identical, and an overwrite rotates the previous five versions away first;
  • - New Save From Map synthesizes a fresh save out of a map you just drew, no base save required.

It is flagged Experimental and it means it. Keep your own backups, and tell me what breaks.

The cockpit

  • - a workspace you rearrange - dockable panels, floating windows, several maps open in tabs, minimap, tile explorer;
  • - fluid zoom and panning, and toggleable real-time tile animation;
  • - every editor action is a typed command - bindable, scriptable and replayable from a console;
  • - a user manual in the Help menu, and a UI that behaves like a desktop application should.
Planned
  • - terrain templates and adjacent-tile suggestions, so building mountains stops being manual work;
  • - custom tile packs, and the tooling to build them;
  • - installing finished maps straight into the game.

... only time will tell what other features might be.
